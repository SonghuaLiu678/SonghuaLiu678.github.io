About

I am a PhD candidate in Economics. My research uses laboratory and survey experiments to study social norms, information credibility, and cooperation in environmental settings. My dissertation examines when social norms succeed in sustaining cooperation, how information frictions cause norms to fail, and what it costs to restore cooperative behavior through policy intervention.

Dissertation

Working title: Boundaries, Failure, and Repair of Social Norms in Environmental Cooperation

My dissertation consists of three complementary chapters. Chapter 1 identifies the conditions under which information credibility sustains norm-based compliance. Chapter 2 (job market paper) uses an incentivized laboratory experiment to show how information frictions generate strategic misreporting and erode trust, undermining cooperative equilibria. Chapter 3 studies the cost of policy interventions that restore compliant behavior—particularly the risk of crowding out intrinsic motivation in high-trust environments.
