Appeal or Audit? Honest Reporting for Environmental Subsidies

Work in progress

Environmental programs rely on information supplied by the regulated party, which the regulator can verify only in part. In a laboratory experiment with 12 sessions, senders report an energy-knowledge score (HIGH or LOW) to a fixed receiver over twelve rounds, and the receiver decides whether to grant a subsidy. Sessions receive a responsibility appeal or a baseline, crossed with a 30% audit or no audit. The experiment compares which of the two raises reporting accuracy.

Solar Geoengineering in the Marketplace: Evidence on Moral Hazard from Consumer Choices

Work in progress

Does the chance to buy a real solar geoengineering product crowd out carbon-offset purchases? In a survey experiment on Prolific with 2,400 participants in three arms, people allocate a $20 budget across personal use, carbon offsets, and cooling credits from Make Sunsets, a commercial stratospheric aerosol injection company. A random subset of decisions is implemented with real money. Comparing the arms separates the effect of information about the technology from the effect of being able to buy it.
