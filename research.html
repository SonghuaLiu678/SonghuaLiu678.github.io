Job Market Paper

Information Frictions, Misreporting, and the Erosion of Trust: An Experimental Study of Norm Failure

with [Coauthor Name(s)]

This paper studies how information frictions cause social norms to fail. In an incentivized laboratory experiment, senders privately observe information and report it to receivers under either an honesty norm or a contribution norm, with or without an audit mechanism. I track misreporting behavior and the trajectory of receivers' trust over repeated interactions to identify the process through which norm-sustained cooperation breaks down.

Working Paper

Information Credibility and the Boundary of Social Norm Compliance

Using a 2×2×2 survey experiment that varies the source, peer reputation, and precision of information, this paper identifies the conditions under which information credibility determines whether a social norm successfully shapes compliance intentions.

Working Paper

The Cost of Policy Intervention: Crowding Out Intrinsic Motivation in High-Trust Environments

This paper examines the cost of policy interventions designed to restore cooperative behavior after norm failure. Using a survey experiment that varies the level of trust and the type of intervention, I study how interventions interact with intrinsic motivation to shape the overall cost-effectiveness of policy.
